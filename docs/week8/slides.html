Question 1

The sales manager of a clothing company claims that the mean spending by customers is less than $80. A sample of 50 customers has a mean spending of $76.25 and a standard deviation of $15.25. We wish to perform an appropriate hypothesis test at the 5% level of significance. a) State the hypotheses b) What is the p-value? c) Interpret the results in the context of the question. d) Is there evidence to support the manager’s claim? e) What is a potential error in the test above?

Source: Workshop8_solutions.pdf, page 11

Question 1_solution

The sales manager of a clothing company reports that the mean spending by customers is less than $80. A sample of 50 customers has a mean spending of $76.25 and a standard deviation of $15.25. We wish to perform an appropriate hypothesis test at the 5% level of significance. (Note: Include all steps to get full marks.) a) State the hypotheses H0: \mu{} = 80 H1: µ < 80 b) What is the p-value? Test statistic = = -1.739 P-value = P(t<-1.739) = T.DIST(-1.739,49,TRUE) =0.044176

Source: Workshop8_solutions.pdf, page 12

Question 1_solution

  1. Interpret the results in the context of the question. We reject the null as the p-value is less than 0.05. There is sufficient evidence at the 5% level of significance that the true mean spending is less than $80. d) Is there evidence to support the manager’s claim? Yes, as per our result in part c, there is evidence to support the manager’s claim. e) What is a potential error in the test above? Interpret the error in the context of the question. Type 1 error where H0 is true and we reject it. We conclude that the true mean spending is less than $80 when it actually is not.

Question 4

A popular weekly magazine asserts that fewer than 40% of households in Australia claim to have changed their lifestyles because of escalating energy prices. Recently, a survey of 180 households was conducted including this question, and 63 of these households claimed to have made lifestyle changes due to escalating energy prices. a) Assuming the data obtained is a random sample, is there evidence to support the magazine’s claim at the 10% level of significance. Use the p-value method. b) Would the result of the test have been different if you had used a 5% significance level? Explain. c) Obtain a 90% confidence interval for the true proportion of Australian households that claim to have made lifestyle changes due to escalating energy prices.

Source: Workshop8_solutions.pdf, page 23

Question 4_solutions

  1. Assuming the data obtained is a random sample, is there evidence to support the magazine’s claim at the 10% level of significance. Use the p-value method. Let X = number of households that made lifestyle change due to increasing energy prices Rule of thumb check: n π = 180x0.40=72 and n(1- π) = 80(0.60)=108 are both > 5 The normal approximation is reasonable. Step 1: Hypothesis: H0: \pi{} = 0.40 (or \pi{} \geq{} 0.40) n=180; X=63; p=63/180 = 0.35; \alpha{}=0.10 H1: \pi{} < 0.40 Step 2: Test Statistic: For \pi{} always use Z

Source: Workshop8_solutions.pdf, page 24

Question 4_solutions

Step 3: p – value So, P(Z<-1.37) =0.0853 =NORM.S.DIST(-1.37,true) Step 4: Decision Rule Reject H0 if p-value < \alpha{}; Since 0.0853 < 0.10, we can reject H0. Step 5: Conclusion There is sufficient evidence at the 10% level of significance that the true proportion of households who have made the change to lifestyle due to increasing energy prices is less than 40%. The data supports the magazine’s claim.
