Past ETX5900 exam coverage against the 2026 eight-week course

Scope and counting rule

Sources: ETX5900 Exam_2026.docx.md (called MD below) and Nov12 Exam_ETX5900.docx.pdf (called PDF below). Each has 10 MCQs, 10 numerical entry questions, and three multi-part short answers, for 23 numbered questions per paper. The MD file marks MCQ keys in bold and gives numerical keys after Answer:; the PDF marks MCQ keys in bold and written answers in red. The answer markings were used to review the items, but not to decide their curriculum tags.

Each numbered question receives one primary topic, week, and learning outcome so the counts add to 23 per paper. Mixed short-answer questions are listed by subpart in their notes; secondary outcomes are mentioned but do not increase the totals. A dash means the item assesses a legacy technique outside the planned 2026 core. Week 8 is integration and revision, so a question using previously taught techniques is assigned to its substantive teaching week rather than counted again in Week 8. These are question counts, not mark-weighted coverage.

Answer-key and item-quality audit before reuse

  • MD Q1: Positive correlation in simple linear regression implies a positive fitted slope. The bolded C says correlation indicates strength but not direction; A is the defensible answer.
  • MD Q11: With standard error 12/√36 = 2, P(X̄ > 33) = P(Z > 1.5) ≈ 0.0668, which rounds to 0.07 to two decimal places, not the marked 0.06.
  • MD Q13: No Answer: entry follows the numerical forecast question. The image-based regression coefficients would need to be captured in a reproducible source before reuse.
  • MD Q19: The displayed 3 × 3 table gives a chi-square statistic of 27.31 (to two decimal places); the marked 36.54 does not match that table.
  • MD Q22(c): The worked coefficient-test answer uses two different p-values (9.42E-15/2 and 2.55E-17) for the same decision. Recheck against the displayed regression output. The wording also says education has a positive effect, which is a causal claim stronger than an observational regression establishes.
  • PDF Q6: The stem says 40 samples were drawn but asks how many of 60 intervals miss the mean. The marked 2 applies to 40; 60 would give 3 in expectation.
  • PDF Q13: The 90th percentile of N(4565, 1063²) is about 5927 dollars, not the red answer 5900.
  • PDF Q16: The point estimate is 140/200 = 0.70, not the red answer 0.43.
  • PDF Q17: For simple regression, R² = 0.85² = 0.7225, so the answer is 72.25%, not the red 56.25%.
  • PDF Q21: The answer repeats H0 for the alternative hypothesis. The reported test statistic −1.4798 and p-value 0.0730 conflict with the stated data: (6.45 − 7)/(1.36/√100) ≈ −4.044 (t with 99 degrees of freedom). Recalculate the p-value and conclusion before reuse.
  • PDF Q22(a): The answer labels 6.45, the observed sample mean, as the mean of the sampling distribution. That theoretical mean is the unknown population mean μ; 6.45 is an estimate of it.

Blueprint implication: These papers strongly favour Weeks 2–4 and preserve 13 legacy items. A 2026 exam needs deliberate Week 1/LO1 and Week 7/LO6 questions, direct simple and multiple regression coverage, and an explicit balance check by marks and subparts. The old keys should not be imported into Moodle without independent verification.
